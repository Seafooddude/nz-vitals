About & methodology

What this is

NZ Vitals is a one-page read on the health of the New Zealand economy: growth, jobs, inflation, interest rates and the exchange rate. It is built as a static website that rebuilds itself from official data, so the numbers refresh without manual work. It is a New Zealand take on the US site Econvitals.

Indicators

Items marked "Coming soon" on the dashboard are not yet connected to official data.

Growth and jobs

Real GDP (quarterly growth). The change in the volume of goods and services New Zealand produces, adjusted for inflation. It is the broadest measure of whether the economy is expanding or shrinking. Source: OECD (seasonally adjusted), distributed by FRED, the St. Louis Fed's data service.

Unemployment rate. The share of the labour force that has no job and is actively looking for one, from the Household Labour Force Survey. It is a lagging indicator: it tends to rise after the economy has already slowed. Source: OECD (seasonally adjusted), distributed by FRED, so it can differ slightly from Stats NZ's own headline figure.

Wage growth. Annual growth in the Labour Cost Index, which tracks what employers pay for the same job over time. Fast wage growth can keep inflation high, while slow growth squeezes household budgets. Source: Stats NZ.

Prices

CPI inflation. The annual change in the Consumers Price Index, the main measure of inflation. The Reserve Bank aims to keep it between 1% and 3% over the medium term, focusing on 2%. Source: OECD (Consumer price indices, New Zealand, growth over one year), which republishes Stats NZ's figures. The shaded band on the chart is the 1–3% target range.

Non-tradables inflation. Inflation in goods and services that are not traded internationally, such as rents, rates, insurance and local services. Because it is driven by domestic demand and costs, the Reserve Bank watches it closely as a sign of underlying inflation pressure. Source: Stats NZ.

House prices. The annual change in house prices. Housing is the largest asset for most New Zealand households, so price changes affect wealth, spending and borrowing. Source: Bank for International Settlements residential property price index, distributed by FRED. It is quarterly and published with a lag, so it runs a few months behind other indicators.

Current account balance. The balance of New Zealand's transactions with the rest of the world: trade in goods and services, investment income paid and received, and transfers. A persistent deficit means New Zealand relies on foreign funding, which can leave the NZ dollar more exposed. Shown in NZ$ billion per quarter, seasonally adjusted. Source: OECD balance of payments.

Confidence

Consumer and business confidence. OECD composite indices built from household and business surveys, scaled so that 100 is the long-run average. Confidence is a leading indicator: spending and hiring decisions often follow how people feel. Source: OECD (amplitude adjusted).

Interest rates and markets

Official Cash Rate (OCR). The Reserve Bank's policy interest rate, set at scheduled reviews during the year. Raising it makes borrowing dearer and cools spending and inflation; cutting it does the opposite. The chart shows every change since 2018, drawn as steps, plus the latest day. Source: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (table B2).

10-year government bond yield. The interest rate the government pays to borrow for ten years, taken from the secondary-market closing yield. It reflects expectations for inflation and the OCR, and it anchors longer-term fixed mortgage rates. The chart shows the last observation of each month, plus the latest day. Source: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (table B2).

NZX 50. An index of the 50 largest companies listed on the New Zealand sharemarket, a quick read on investor confidence. Planned source: NZX.

The NZ dollar and trade

NZD/USD. US dollars per New Zealand dollar. A weaker NZ dollar makes imports dearer, which tends to lift inflation, and helps exporters. Source: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (table B1).

NZ dollar (TWI). The trade-weighted index measures the NZ dollar against a basket of 17 currencies, weighted by trade with each country. It gives a fuller picture than any single exchange rate. Source: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (table B1).

Dairy price (GDT). The Global Dairy Trade whole milk powder price in US dollars per tonne. Dairy is New Zealand's largest export, so this price influences farm incomes, export earnings and the NZ dollar. Planned source: Global Dairy Trade.

Regions

Earnings by region. Median hourly and weekly earnings of people in paid employment in each region, with the New Zealand figure as a benchmark. Hourly earnings are the fairer comparison between regions because they do not depend on how many hours people work. This covers earnings from paid employment only, not benefits or investment income, and the figures are survey estimates, so smaller regions are noisier. Some regions are grouped together in the source data. Source: Stats NZ, Aotearoa Data Explorer (dataset INC_INC_001), updated once a year.

Population by region. The number of people living in each region at 30 June 2023 (the newest figure in Stats NZ's subnational population projections, which use 2023 as the base year), and the projected population every ten years to 2053. The projections use Stats NZ's medium scenario. They show what would happen if the assumptions about births, deaths and migration hold, so they are not forecasts, and they get less certain the further ahead they look. Stats NZ rounds them to the nearest hundred. The 16 regional councils are combined into the same 12 regions as the earnings map: Nelson, Tasman, Marlborough and the West Coast are one region, and so are Gisborne and Hawke's Bay.

Region pages. Click any region on a map or in a list to open its own page. It shows that region's earnings back to 1998 beside the New Zealand figure, how far above or below New Zealand it has been over time, how many people live there now and are projected to by 2053, and where it ranks among the 12 regions. Earnings are for people in paid employment, for the year to June, and come from a Stats NZ survey, so small regions can move around from year to year.

How the data is handled

  • A scheduled job downloads the official files, checks each value is plausible, and rebuilds the site. If a download or check fails, the last good data stays on the page.
  • Daily series are summarised as the last observation of each month, plus the latest day.
  • Series are replaced in full on each update, so revisions made by the source agencies flow through.

Limitations

Data is revised by the source agencies, and releases can arrive later than the date shown. If the page cannot refresh, a notice appears when the newest figure is more than ten days old. This site is for general information only and is not financial advice.
